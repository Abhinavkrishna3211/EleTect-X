# Boar deterrence: evidence base for ADR 0023's horn content

Written 2026-09-03, the boar counterpart to `elephant-deterrence-behavioral-science.md`. Scope is
narrower than that document by design: ADR 0023 reuses Elephant's LED patterns for Boar unchanged
(no boar-specific light research was needed to make that call), so this document is almost entirely
about the horn — what wild boar can hear, what plays, and what evidence exists for why. Research pass,
not exhaustive. States confidence honestly per claim, same convention as the elephant document; flag
anything below that turns out wrong on closer reading rather than trusting it blindly.

## 1. The primary evidence: triggered predator-vocalization playback reduces boar crop damage

**Well-established, and boar-specific:** Widén, Clinchy, Felton, Hofmeester, Kuijper, Singh, Widemo,
Zanette & Cromsigt, 2022, *Agriculture, Ecosystems and Environment* 328:107853, "Playbacks of Predator
Vocalizations Reduce Crop Damage by Ungulates." Seven wheat fields in Södermanland, south-central
Sweden, camera traps wired to speakers that fired only when a passing animal triggered the camera —
the same triggered-not-continuous design this device's own horn already uses (ADR 0022's watch window,
fired once per confirmed encounter). Wild boar was one of five ungulate species in the study (with
fallow deer, roe deer, moose and red deer). Predator playbacks (wolf howl, dog bark, human voice)
reduced patch use and crop damage more than control sounds (owl, goose, raven).
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167880922000020

**The direct relevance, and the direct gap.** This is the single best boar-specific playback-efficacy
result in the literature review below, and it is why ADR 0023 keeps Boar inside the predator-growl
horn category rather than inventing a new one. But its three stimuli were wolf, dog and human — this
device carries neither. What it plays for Boar is tiger and lion, already provisioned for Elephant
(ADR 0015/0016). The next two sections are what closes that gap, and the honest note is that "closes"
means "makes it an evidenced ecological substitution," not "makes it a replication."

## 2. Tiger and leopard as real Sus scrofa predators on the Indian subcontinent

**Well-established, multi-source, direct diet data:** wild boar is a measured, substantial component
of tiger and leopard diet across Indian and Nepali reserves, not an incidental prey item.

- Bardia National Park, Nepal: wild boar contributed 6.98% of tiger diet biomass and 15.72% of leopard
  diet biomass.
  https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24750263.2025.2542465
- Nagarjunasagar Srisailam Tiger Reserve, India: scat analysis found wild boar the *most common* prey
  item, ahead of chital and sambar.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S161650470470138X
- Multiple reviews across the Indian subcontinent name wild boar among the medium-sized prey species
  (with chital, sambar, hog deer) that make up the bulk of tiger diet regionally.

This is the ecological basis ADR 0023 leans on for reusing Elephant's tiger/lion tracks on Boar: unlike
a European wolf recording, a tiger or leopard vocalization is a real, locally-relevant predator cue for
this exact prey species at this exact site. It is an inference from predator ecology, not a playback
result — Section 3 is what turns part of that inference into a measured number.

## 3. Direct evidence: Amur tiger call playback tested against real wild boar

**Well-established, boar-specific, and specifically about tiger calls — found and read this session,
correcting an earlier assumption that this source was inaccessible.** Ani (2025) 15(7):1017,
"Comparing Durations of Different Countermeasure Efficacies Against Wild Boar (*Sus scrofa*) in
Cornfields of Hunchun, Jilin Province, China" (2016–2021 field seasons). Hunchun is a key Amur tiger
range in China, which is precisely why this study used real Amur tiger calls as one of its auditory
treatments. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11987724/ (DOI 10.3390/ani15071017)

Five deterrent categories compared: visual (solar blinkers, ribbons), auditory (predator calls),
tactile (electric fencing), olfactory (tiger feces), and combinations. Measured outcome was days of
sustained crop-damage prevention before efficacy declined (habituation, functionally).

| Treatment | Duration before decline |
|---|---|
| Red solar blinkers (1000 mA) | 32.25 ± 4.22 days |
| Electric fencing (three wires) | 29.67 ± 0.58 days |
| Yellow solar blinkers (1000 mA) | 29.67 ± 4.58 days |
| Green solar blinkers (1000 mA) | 29.58 ± 5.60 days |
| Tiger feces + predator calls | 27.34 ± 2.94 days |
| Adult Amur tiger calls (15s) + wild boar distress calls (15s) | 26.50 ± 2.38 days |

Overall category ranking (higher = more durable): tactile 152.56 > visual 118.29 > auditory 72.60.
Red solar blinkers had the best cost-effectiveness ratio (0.31) of any single treatment; electric
fencing had the longest absolute durability (rated as lasting years with maintenance) but the worst
cost-effectiveness ratio (0.027) in this comparison.

**Read this plainly, and read it carefully — it is not "light beats sound for this device."** Every
treatment in this table, auditory included, ran as *continuous, unconditional* exposure across a field
for weeks at a time. That is exactly the condition Section 1's Widén study identifies as the driver of
habituation, and exactly the condition its own triggered-camera-trap design exists to avoid. This
device's horn is not a continuous broadcast — ADR 0022's watch window fires the tier once per confirmed
encounter, the same triggered paradigm Widén used, not Hunchun's continuous one. So the honest reading
of this table is: **continuous auditory broadcast habituates faster than continuous visual broadcast,
in this environment, over these five weeks** — a real and useful finding, and the reason ADR 0023
records it rather than the "unread, paywalled" placeholder an earlier pass assumed — but it is evidence
about exposure schedule, not a verdict on triggered predator-call playback specifically, which this
study did not isolate as its own condition. A genuine boar-specific *triggered* light-vs-sound
comparison remains the open gap (see ADR 0023's Follow-ups).

**What this section does add, directly:** a real, quantified, tiger-call-on-wild-boar result — 26.5
days of measured deterrence from Amur tiger playback specifically. That is a stronger evidentiary basis
for ADR 0023's tiger track than the ecological-inference argument in Section 2 alone, even accounting
for the continuous-exposure caveat above, because the animal, the predator species played, and the
behavioural outcome (reduced field visitation) all match this device's use case directly.

## 4. What wild boar can hear

**Well-established for domestic pig, applied to wild boar by close genetic relationship, not measured
in wild boar directly:** Heffner & Heffner, 1990, *Hearing Research*, "Hearing in domestic pigs (Sus
scrofa) and goats (Capra hircus)." Behavioral audiograms on three pigs: hearing range 42 Hz–40.5 kHz,
best sensitivity 250 Hz–16 kHz. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2272932/

No search in this pass found an audiogram measured in wild-caught *Sus scrofa* specifically —
domestic pig is the same species, bred for production traits rather than hearing, so the audiogram is
a reasonable proxy but not a wild-boar-confirmed number. Mark this "plausible-by-analogy," same
confidence tier the elephant document gives its own domestic-analogue claims.

**Implication for the horn:** the tiger/lion tracks already provisioned for Elephant sit well inside
this range — animal vocalizations of this kind carry substantial energy in the few-hundred-Hz to
several-kHz band, squarely inside a pig's region of best sensitivity. No frequency-specific tuning is
needed or attempted for Boar; this is a confirmation that the existing content is audible to the
target, not a finding that changes anything about ADR 0023's decision.

## 5. What wild boar can see

**Well-established for domestic pig (same species), not measured in wild boar directly:** Neitz &
Jacobs, 1989, *Visual Neuroscience*, "Spectral sensitivity of cones in an ungulate." ERG flicker
photometry found two cone classes in the pig retina, peak sensitivity at 439 nm and 556 nm — the
retinal basis for dichromatic (blue/green) color vision, similar in kind to the elephant document's
own dichromacy finding for elephants.
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/abs/spectral-sensitivity-of-cones-in-an-ungulate/1BC07A10BDF59B9D630C7A73B5837CAB

**Plausible-by-analogy, not from a controlled study:** wild boar are widely described (general wildlife
references, not a peer-reviewed acuity study located in this pass) as having comparatively weak
eyesight relative to their smell and hearing — one commonly repeated claim is difficulty recognizing a
standing human at 10–15 m. Treat this the way the elephant document treats its own unsourced-strength
claims: plausible, not a number to cite as measured.

**No implication for this design.** ADR 0023 reuses Elephant's LED patterns for Boar unchanged; nothing
in this section motivated a change, and none was made. It is recorded here only because it was the
natural counterpart to Section 4, and because a future decision to give Boar its own LED pattern (not
currently planned) would start from this section rather than from nothing.

## 6. Habituation, and why the triggered paradigm matters twice over

Two independent sources in this document converge on the same mechanism from different directions:

- Section 1 (Widén et al. 2022): triggered-only playback, camera-trap-gated, was the explicit design
  choice made *to minimize habituation* relative to continuous exposure.
- Section 3 (Ani 2025 / Hunchun): continuous exposure across weeks showed auditory deterrents declining
  faster than visual ones — evidence, from the other direction, that exposure schedule is doing real
  work in these outcomes.

This device's horn was already triggered-only for Elephant (ADR 0022's bounded watch window, one fire
per confirmed encounter) before Boar was ever in scope, for reasons ADR 0017/0022 state independently
(latency, power, and `cognition/bandit.py`'s own habituation model). Boar inherits that same triggered
design with no separate justification needed — but Section 3 is a reminder that the mechanism this
project already chose is doing real, literature-supported work, not just a latency/power convenience.

## 7. Evidence-tier summary, mapped to ADR 0023's decisions

| ADR 0023 decision | Evidence tier |
|---|---|
| Boar stays in the predator-growl content category (no bee track) | Well-established negative: King et al. 2007's bee-aversion mechanism (stinging near eyes/trunk) has no boar analogue — absence of evidence for a boar-specific mechanism, not evidence against bees working on boar for some other reason. |
| Tiger/lion tracks reused for Boar | Two tiers: ecological inference (Section 2, well-established diet data) *plus* a direct measured result using actual tiger calls on actual wild boar (Section 3, well-established but paradigm-caveated). |
| No firecracker/bang pool for Boar at Tier 3 | Design consistency (household-proximity gate applies trivially), not a boar-specific finding. |
| LED patterns reused unchanged for Boar | Not evidence-backed for Boar specifically — carried over from Elephant's own weaker-than-audio LED evidence base (ADR 0014), per Section 5's "no implication" note. |
| Household Tier-3 gate applies unchanged to Boar | Explicitly not evidence-backed for Boar — the null result that motivates it (Hedges & Gunaryadi 2010) is an elephant study. Consistency choice, stated as such in ADR 0023. |
| Triggered-not-continuous firing | Now supported from two directions (Sections 1 and 3), not just carried over from Elephant's own rationale. |
